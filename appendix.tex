Three entries in Table~\ref{tab:ppo-settings} may need explanation. The reward is written with the scale factor $\kappa$ and drive $D=|h|/25$ (Section~\ref{sec:hrl}); together they give a reward of 0.2 for each unit $h$ moves toward the setpoint. The policy makes a decision every 10 ticks, so the discount from one decision to the next is $0.99^{10}\approx0.90$. Checkpoint selection describes how we picked the snapshot of each policy that was kept (Section~\ref{sec:training}): for example, an HRL snapshot with a mean $|h|$ of 15 on the eight test trials would be kept over one with a mean of 20.

\subsection{Mechanism checks and sensitivity analysis}\label{app:additional}

Table~\ref{tab:main} gives results that the main text refers to but does not show. The mechanism checks ask why agents are trapped. For example, precise beliefs raised the fraction of HRL agents trapped by 0.24, from 0.46 with low-precision beliefs to 0.70. The sensitivity analysis asks whether the benefit of decanalization depends on the setting chosen for the main comparison. For HRL agents it does not: decanalization lowered MAE at every dose and boost tested, by 43 to 98 points. The size of the benefit varied, and a boost of 10 helped less than a boost of 3 and was costly during the intervention itself (MAE 108, against 62). Medians show the typical trial, which is less affected by the few trials with very large deficits.
