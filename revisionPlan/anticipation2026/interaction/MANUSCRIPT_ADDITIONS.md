# Proposed factorial sensitivity addition

The manuscript source is preserved. Keep the original k=1/half-floor primary condition.

> To test whether post-decanalization count retention modified the effect of accelerated traversal counting, we crossed increments of one and seven during decanalization with resetting, halving and flooring, or retaining counts at recanalization. Each cell contained 250 trials under the same fixed HRL policy. We compared the 7-minus-1 rate effects between retention rules using four-cell heteroskedastic contrasts and Welch-Satterthwaite intervals. Holm correction covered three rule contrasts and two endpoints. Same-ID and baseline-change contrasts were sensitivity analyses.

> The factorial evaluation did not establish an interaction between counting rate and retention rule for post-intervention absolute deviation or received reward after multiplicity correction. This leaves interaction effects uncertain rather than establishing independence or equivalence.

> Retaining all counts rather than halving and flooring changed the 7-minus-1 counting-rate effect by +0.313 [-4.178, 4.803] for absolute deviation and -1.467 [-4.859, 1.924] for received reward (95% confidence intervals; Holm p=1 and 1, respectively).

Report the full interaction table and uncertainty, plus any sensitivity disagreement. A significant comparison with CCC under one rule and an uncertain comparison under another is not itself an interaction. These are environmental-history manipulations under one trained policy; they do not establish neural plasticity or generalization across training runs.
